- [persona] About 492 of 499 visitors identified missing pricing or cost information, so publish an indicative price range or a self-service pricing route before asking them to contact sales.
- [persona] About 430 of 499 visitors needed relevant customer references before proceeding, so add named case studies with measurable outcomes from comparable industries, company sizes, and use cases.
- [persona] About 116 of 499 visitors found “Vi digitaliserar byggbranschen” written for someone else, so create clearer role- and industry-specific entry points and explain specialist terms in plain language.
- [persona] About 334 of 499 visitors needed setup-time information, while about 271 of 499 needed integration details and about 159 of 499 needed hardware details, so explain implementation requirements next to the relevant solutions.

**Read with care: product or company understanding, trust did not separate visitors.** Nearly everyone gave the same answer, so the average is a property of the page rather than a difference between visitor kinds. For trust in particular the middle anchor — a credible company whose claims are unproven for my case — is simply true of any first visit, so the rating cannot move. What trust *does* vary on is what visitors will commit to and which claims they accept, both in section 3c.

**What the scores mean (anchors given to every persona):**

- **Product or company understanding** — 1: I still could not say what they sell. 3: I know the category, but not what it would do for me. 5: I could explain to a colleague what it does and for whom.
- **Language and relevance** — 1: written for someone else; nothing from my world. 3: general business language with some familiar examples. 5: my situations and my words; I recognised my own problems.
- **Perceived practical value** — 1: no idea what would change in my day. 3: a plausible benefit, not concrete. 5: I can name what would get easier or cheaper for me.
- **Trust** — 1: I would not share my data with them. 3: a credible company, but the claims are unproven for my case. 5: I would put my own operation on it without asking for references.
- **Confidence in the next step** — 1: no idea what I should do next or what would happen. 3: I see a button but not what follows. 5: I know exactly what happens after clicking, and it suits me.
- **Ease of completing that step** — 1: the step is unclear, demanding or off-putting. 3: doable, with some friction or uncertainty. 5: quick and obvious, nothing in the way.

## 9. Method, coverage and limits

- **Personas:** 999 synthetic visitors drawn proportionally from the six visitor kinds in the brief (§4) with Swedish construction-sector roles, each carrying its own reason for visiting (a specific problem today, or exploring) and prior familiarity with Infobric. They were not shown the analytics.
- **Visit:** each persona briefed itself, opened the page in a real browser, judged the first screen (with leaving as a valid outcome), read the whole page, clicked the primary button once, then answered the questionnaire and rated the six dimensions on the anchors above.
- **Coverage:** 1000 of 1000 visits completed, 999 passed the automated checks (100%); persona fidelity 100%; 0.96 of quoted phrases were found verbatim on the page (grounding check).
- **Two readings of every score:** in-browse ratings cluster on the polite middle for some dimensions (a known effect of rating at the end of a long browsing session); the scored-after-the-visit reading has the same persona re-rate from its own notes. Where both agree, the result is robust; where they differ, §1 shows both.
